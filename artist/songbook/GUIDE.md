Every song in `artist/songbook/*.toml` is a complete song written by a person (or by Claude
writing as a songwriter), used once, never re-rolled from parts. Every song is built on one of
the two models below. Read both songs in full first (their lyrics are at the end of this file).

## Model A: Outrun Your Shadow (the dense rap model)

Rap over hard techno, 150 BPM, 4:36. Measured: 330 words, 1,722 characters; vocals on about
40 percent of the track, the rest is music.

| Section | Lines | Syllables a line | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 1 | 14 | the title as a question: "can you outrun your own shadow?" |
| Verse 1 | 10 | 20 | the chase: the past, scars, being buried and climbing out |
| Hook | 1 | 7 | one short question the crowd can feel: "Can you feel the madness rise?" |
| Drop | 1 | 5 | a chant over the music: "Run faster, deeper" |
| Verse 2 | 10 | 18 | escalation: the cage, the riot, taking the throne |
| Outro | 5 | 5 | short spoken lines spaced over the last drops, the twist |

How it is built:

1. **The title is a question, not a chorus.** It is asked once at the start and answered at
   the end ("Look in the mirror / Who are you running from? / There is no escape"). The
   listener carries the question through the whole song.
2. **Double-time verses, one bar per line.** Every verse line is 16 to 22 syllables, which is
   one bar of sixteenth notes at 150 to 175 BPM. Lines are long and full, never short and
   padded. Commas mark the breath inside the bar.
3. **Rhyme chains, not couplets.** One vowel sound runs for three or four lines, then the
   chain switches: break / take / flame / grave / ache (long a), then night / lies / eyes (long
   i), then boil / soil, then drawn / gone. Inside the lines the words rhyme too ("the noise
   and the fake and the lies", "the hunger ... the drive ... to survive").
4. **Physical, violent pictures.** Blades, a grave, a flame, blood that boils, soil, curtains
   drawn, a cage, glass, ashes, a throne, a mirror. Feelings are shown as things.
5. **The song knows its own tempo.** "My heartbeat is faster, a hundred and fifty" ties the
   words to the beat the listener hears.
6. **Verse 2 raises the stakes.** Verse 1 is running and hurt; verse 2 is rage and taking
   control ("they wanted me broken, they wanted me quiet, I walked in the room and I started a
   riot"). Same flow, higher energy.
7. **Space.** Hook and drop are a few words; long stretches are only music. The words land
   hard because they are not everywhere.

## Model B: Kindness Needs Truth (the argument model)

Sung rap, 90 to 95 BPM (half time, so it sits under a 180 BPM kick), 4:10. Measured: about 600
words, 3,300 characters, three full verses.

| Section | Lines | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 2 | an image: "I keep a list of every good thing I never got paid for" |
| Verse 1 | 16 | the situation and the conflict: heart says one thing, mind another |
| Chorus | 8 | the thesis, ending in the title idea: "love needs a limit, compassion needs truth" |
| Verse 2 | 16 | the backstory and the lesson learned |
| Chorus | 8 | repeated |
| Bridge | 6 | the turn: short, plain declarations, a different rhythm |
| Verse 3 | 16 | the new rules for living, the resolution |
| Final Chorus | 8 | the chorus rewritten by what happened ("it hurts when the world takes" becomes "I won't let the world take") |
| Outro | 4 | back to the opening image, closed |

How it is built:

1. **One real tension, argued honestly.** Kindness against self-respect. Neither side is a
   straw man; the song changes its mind as it goes.
2. **Couplets that rhyme loosely.** Two lines, one thought, a slant rhyme: receipt / weak,
   ledge / head, distance / existence, weight / faith. 12 to 16 syllables a line.
3. **Devices a listener remembers.** Repetition with a twist ("held the elevator, held my
   tongue, held a friend from the ledge, held a promise like a match"); the inner voices quoted
   ("My heart says, 'Give them grace'", "My mind says, 'Read the pattern'"); questions
   ("Was I kind, or was I scared they would leave if I said no?"); opposites (a judge and a
   son, mercy and a boundary); a line that works as a proverb ("love needs a limit,
   compassion needs truth").
4. **Small true details.** "Drove home silent in a borrowed coat", "a storm in my throat".
5. **The chorus states the point, then the final chorus earns a new version of it.**
6. **The outro closes the loop** with the image from the intro.

## What both songs share (every song here does all of it)

- One central question or tension, named in the title.
- A speaker who is a person with a past, talking to someone (you, me, a mirror).
- Verses carry the story and are dense; hooks are short and come back.
- Concrete pictures instead of abstract words.
- An arc: the end is different from the start (an answer, a decision, a twist).
- Rhyme that serves the meaning; slant rhyme is fine, a forced rhyme never is.
- A title of 1 to 4 words that is the song's hook (see Themes and titles).

So every song now:

1. **Has a universal theme a whole dance floor can feel at once**, from one of eight families
   (the song's `family` field; never two of the same family in a row):
   - `awakening`: something wakes inside, a threshold, a first spark (Signal Detected).
   - `defiance`: war on the rules, taking it back, refusing to kneel (Project Mayhem).
   - `inner-demons`: the dark inside, faced instead of run from (Dancing With Demons, Outrun Your Shadow).
   - `drive`: action, momentum, no more waiting (Then Do It, Let's Fucking Go).
   - `devotion`: holding on to someone or something through the night (Stay With Me).
   - `escape`: breaking out, leaving the cage, the open road at full speed.
   - `ritual`: the crowd, the night, the drop as a ceremony, bodies moving as one.
   - `truth`: a hard lesson said plainly, a thesis (Kindness Needs Truth).
2. **Is sung by "I" to "you"** (a lover, an enemy, the crowd, the mirror), in the present tense.
   Concrete physical pictures (blood, fire, glass, rain, a door, a mirror, a heartbeat, a
   floor shaking), never biography: no family members, jobs, prices, cities, brands or
   household objects. The listener must be able to put their own life into it.
3. **Has a title of 1 to 4 words that is its hook**, sung or chanted at least twice, in one of
   these shapes: a command (Then Do It, Stay With Me, Outrun Your Shadow), a cinematic noun
   phrase (Project Mayhem, Pressure Spiral, Silent Transmission), an event (Signal Detected,
   Crossing The Threshold), a gerund with an object (Dancing With Demons) or a thesis
   (Kindness Needs Truth). Never a question, never a name, never a number. Strong, physical
   words: burn, break, rise, run, war, fire, blood, pressure, threshold, mayhem, shadow.
4. **Still follows its model** (A: the dense chase, B: the argument) and the shared rules above.

`anchor/songbook.py` (`title_problems`) checks every title against these rules and the tests
fail a book that breaks them. Released songs keep their names.

## Choosing the model

- `rap` and `hiphop` songs, and hard `revolt` or `story` songs in any group: **Model A**.
  Two verses of 8 to 12 lines, 16 to 22 syllables each; a one or two line hook; a chant
  drop; a whispered outro of 3 to 5 short lines. 1,300 to 2,000 characters.
- `argument`, `confession`, `letter` songs: **Model B**. Two or three verses of 8 to 16
  lines, 12 to 16 syllables each; a 4 to 8 line chorus; a bridge; a rewritten final chorus;
  an outro that returns to the intro. 1,800 to 3,000 characters.
- `celebration`, `dialogue`, `vibe`, `mantra` and most `hardtech` / `psy` songs borrow from
  either, but keep the shared rules. `vibe` songs stay short on purpose.

Write the model in the song's `model` field ("A" or "B"). The robot gives a song more time when
its lyrics are long, so a Model B song is not squeezed.

## Forms (vary them; never two of the same form in a row)

- `story`: characters, a place, something happens, an ending.
- `revolt`: defiance against something specific (a rule, a boss, a town, a voice in your head).
- `argument`: two sides inside one person (the Kindness Needs Truth form).
- `confession`: something hard admitted, quietly, then loudly.
- `letter`: addressed to one person, past or future.
- `celebration`: pure joy with a reason (a win, a reunion, a first), still concrete.
- `vibe`: mostly music; a few lines, a mood, a chant. Short lyrics on purpose.
- `dialogue`: two voices, two points of view, answering each other.
- `mantra`: Sanskrit shloka or chant as the spine. Psy songs only, at most 1 in 6 psy songs,
  and only where the subject is spiritual (stillness, letting go, the self).

## Genre voices

- `hardtech` (hard bounce, rawstyle bounce, hard groove, hyper acid): sung or shouted English,
  festival scale, a hook a crowd can scream back.
- `psy` (psychedelic, acid psy): hypnotic, inward, images of light, water, time, the body;
  whispered or half-sung verses; mantras rarely.
- `rap` (rap techno, psy rap, hard tech rap): Model A bars. Internal rhyme, rhyme chains,
  punchlines, a point of view. Wacky gangster swagger, hard and heavy, or storytelling.
- `hiphop` (hip hop techno, psy hip hop): story bars and heavy bars mixed, a sung hook.

## Syncing (how the words meet the beat)

- One lyric line is one bar. At 160 to 200 BPM a bar is 1.2 to 1.5 seconds: a Model A line
  of 16 to 22 syllables is double time; a Model B line of 12 to 16 syllables is half time.
- Put the stressed words on the beat: start lines with the important word, end them on the
  rhyme.
- Leave instrumental `[Drop]` sections empty or with one short chant. The video shows each
  line as it is sung, so a line the singer cannot fit becomes a line out of sync.
- Section tags stay bare (`[Verse 1]`, not `[Verse 1: whispered]`): a direction inside a tag
  gets sung. The robot strips them.

## Voices (caption line)

Each song names one real human voice the way a session singer is booked: gender, register,
texture, delivery and flow, e.g. "gritty male baritone rapper, double-time flow locked to the
kick, real breaths". Never: robotic, vocoder, auto-tuned, text-to-speech, AI voice, monotone.
Never two songs in a row with the same voice.

## Never

- Robots, machines, systems, circuits, code, signals, data, neon-as-filler, "the void".
- Lines that could sit in any other song ("feel the energy", "lose control tonight").
- The same opening, the same hook pattern, or the same rhyme chain in songs close together.
- Em or en dashes (use a hyphen or a comma).
- A title already used on the channel or that is an existing well-known song.

### Kindness Needs Truth

    [Intro]
    I keep a list of every good thing I never got paid for
    Not in ink, just in the way I still open the same door

    [Verse 1]
    I was taught that a hand out should never need a receipt
    So I gave mine till the skin on my knuckles turned weak
    Held the elevator, held my tongue, held a friend from the ledge
    Held a promise like a match while the rain hit my head
    They call it noble when they watch it from a distance
    But nobody counts the nights I questioned my existence
    Was I kind, or was I scared they would leave if I said no?
    Was I fair, or just afraid of becoming what they hate most?
    My heart says, "Give them grace, everybody's carrying weight"
    My mind says, "Read the pattern, they keep using your faith"
    One sees a wounded person, one sees a loaded gun
    Both believe they're trying to keep me from what I'll become
    So I stand between the mercy and the boundary line
    With a voice in my chest and a case in my mind
    If I do the right thing and it costs me my peace
    Does the cost make it wrong, or is that what it means?

    [Chorus]
    I don't do good for a medal, I do good 'cause I can
    But it hurts when the world takes the heart from your hand
    I can think like a judge, I can feel like a son
    Tryna honor the truth when the two never run as one
    If nobody claps, I'll still know what I chose
    But I won't let my kindness become somebody's throne
    I'm learning love needs a limit, compassion needs truth
    The brain says protect me, the heart says stay true

    [Verse 2]
    There were days I said yes with a storm in my throat
    Then I drove home silent in a borrowed coat
    They got what they wanted, I got better at hiding
    Put a smile on my face while my ribs were colliding
    That isn't fairness, that's a debt with a friendly disguise
    That's calling self-erasure a virtue in someone's eyes
    I confused being needed with being respected
    Let a thousand small wounds go completely unprotected
    Now I listen before I leap, but I don't freeze in the doubt
    I can care about your pain and still ask you to get out
    I can forgive what you did without giving you the keys
    I can wish you a future that has nothing to do with me
    The brain builds a fortress, says, "Never be fooled"
    The heart sees the child underneath what got cruel
    Maybe wisdom isn't choosing which voice gets control

    No reward in the room, no witness, no proof
    Just me and the choice I am making in truth

    If I'm distant, don't assume I forgot how to feel

    I can care what you need without bleeding some more

    [Verse 3]
    I used to think fairness meant everybody gets mine
    Now I know fair can mean drawing a clear line
    Not every apology deserves a return
    Not every lesson requires another chance to be learned
    And I'm still gonna help when the help is sincere
    Still gonna speak when the silence feeds fear
    Still gonna choose the humane road, even unpaid
    But the person I save can't be the person I trade
    So if kindness is a seed, I'll plant it with care
    Not throw every flower at a fist in the air
    Let the brain check the facts, let the heart check the cost
    Let them both tell the truth about what could be lost

    I am more than the service I give when I break

    And I won't let my kindness become somebody's throne
